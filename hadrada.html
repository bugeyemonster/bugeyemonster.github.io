Overview: The Last Great Viking

This application explores the life of Harald Hardrada, one of history's most formidable figures, often called "the last great Viking." His life was a sweeping saga of exile, warfare, and power that spanned from Scandinavia to the heart of the Byzantine Empire and culminated in a fateful invasion of England.

The Long Exile (1030–1045)

At age 15, Harald fought with his half-brother, King Olaf II, at the Battle of Stiklestad. Olaf was killed, and Harald was forced to flee Norway. This began a 15-year odyssey as a mercenary and military commander that would make him one of the wealthiest and most feared warriors of his time. This journey took him across continents, from the fledgling states of Rus' to the opulent heart of the Byzantine Empire.

Commander of the Varangian Guard

Harald's career peaked in Constantinople, where he rose to command the Varangian Guard—an elite unit of Norsemen who served as the personal bodyguards of the Byzantine Emperor. He fought in campaigns across the Mediterranean, in Sicily, Bulgaria, and the Holy Land, amassing an enormous fortune that he shipped back to Yaroslav in Kievan Rus' for safekeeping. This wealth would later fund his claim to the Norwegian throne.

King of Norway (1046–1066)

Harald returned to Norway in 1045, his reputation and wealth so great that his nephew, King Magnus the Good, was forced to make him co-ruler. When Magnus died a year later, Harald became the sole King of Norway. His reign was marked by the centralization of power, the founding of the city of Oslo, and a long, brutal war that directly addresses your query's confusion.

The War Against the Danes

Far from being a Dane, Harald Hardrada spent nearly two decades (1048–1064) fighting a relentless war *against* the King of Denmark, Sweyn II. Harald believed he had a claim to the Danish throne through his nephew Magnus, but the Danes refused to accept him. This long, attritional war, consisting mostly of brutal coastal raids, defined much of his reign and cemented the animosity between the two kingdoms. He ultimately failed to conquer Denmark, signing a peace treaty with Sweyn in 1064, just two years before his death.

The Fateful Year: 1066

With peace secured with Denmark, Hardrada turned his attention to a greater prize: England. When King Edward the Confessor died in January 1066, Hardrada laid claim to the throne, launching a massive invasion of northern England. This decision set the stage for one of the most famous and chaotic years in history, marked by three kings vying for one crown. His invasion is often seen as the climactic end of the Viking Age.
