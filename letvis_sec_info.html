2. Guiding Principles: Critical Standards

The assessment is not a typical IT audit. It is anchored in critical infrastructure and aviation safety standards, where system integrity and availability are paramount.

NIST SP 800-30

Risk Assessment

Provides the core 4-step process: Prepare, Conduct, Communicate, and Maintain. Ensures a structured and repeatable risk determination.

NIST SP 800-82

ICS/OT Security

The non-interference mandate. Demands assessors understand OT safety risks and focus on "fail in known state" compensating controls.

RTCA DO-326A

Airworthiness Security

The safety integration mandate. Links cyber vulnerabilities directly to safety consequences, ensuring risk is prioritized by operational impact.

3. The 8-Day Execution Plan

The 8-day timeline is a parallel effort, front-loading passive data collection (Days 1-4) and dedicating the second half to analysis, correlation, and reporting (Days 5-8).

Day 1: Kickoff & Prep

A1: Finalize Scope, Review SSP. A2: Prepare PCAP collection (Wireshark/Zeek).

Day 2: Interviews & Config

A1: Interview Core System Owner. A2: Execute Configuration Baseline Collection (Ansible).

Day 3: Deep Dive & PCAP

A1: Interview URDI/FDP Managers. A2: Initiate Full Packet Capture on URDI segments.

Day 4: Network Analysis

A1: Interview Network/Security Teams. A2: Deep Dive PCAP Analysis (ASTERIX/ADS-B).

Day 5: Correlation

A1: Review Hardening Results. A2: Correlate Nessus Patch Reports (Plugin 66334).

Day 6: Draft Findings

A1: Draft Findings, Prioritize Risk. A2: Compile & Normalize Technical Evidence.

Day 7: Finalize Report

A1: Draft Recommendations (Policy). A2: Develop System Playbook Templates.

Day 8: Delivery

A1: Stakeholder Briefing. A2: Finalize Remediation Action Plan.

4. Assessment Methods & Focus

The zero-impact mandate requires a focus on passive data collection and process verification. The team's effort is split between technical analysis (network and config) and human analysis (interviews and documentation).

Assessment Effort Breakdown

Key Data Collection Pillars

Network Packet Analysis (30%)

Passively capture traffic (Wireshark, Zeek) to find anomalies and analyze insecure protocols like ASTERIX/ADS-B.

Config & Log Review (30%)

Validate system hardening (Ansible), patch status (Nessus reports), and file integrity (FIM logs).

System Owner Interviews (25%)

Validate documented controls, DR plans, and change management maturity with key personnel.

5. Core Focus Areas (The Playbooks)

To ensure depth, the assessment uses three specific "playbooks" to audit the most critical components of the LETVIS architecture.

Network Infrastructure

Focuses on segmentation efficacy (IT vs. OT), passive data flow mapping, and verifying compensating controls for insecure protocols (ASTERIX).

LETVIS Core (UNIX/Linux)

Focuses on COTS hardening, auditing user privileges, validating log management, and correlating patch status with FIM logs.

URDI & FDP Integrity

The safety-critical focus. Prioritizes data integrity, input validation, access control on FDP binaries, and failover/redundancy testing.

6. Beyond the Obvious: Critical Gaps

A technical audit is incomplete. The plan focuses on identifying systemic gaps that represent the highest risk to operational safety.

⚠️

Safety/Cyber Integration

The DO-326A gap. Risk must be scored by safety impact, not just CVSS. A data integrity vulnerability is a safety hazard.

🔗

External Interfaces

The ADS-B gap. A hardened system is still vulnerable if it ingests corrupted data from unauthenticated external feeds.

🔒

Physical Security

The foundational gap. Unsecured network closets or consoles bypass all digital defenses and must be part of the review.

📜

Policy & Governance

The maturity gap. Technical flaws are symptoms of policy failure, like no continuous monitoring cadence or OT-specific training.

7. Risk & Actionable Recommendations

The final output is not just a list of findings, but a prioritized, safety-weighted risk matrix and a strategic roadmap for building sustainable resilience.

Safety-Weighted Risk Matrix

Risk Level Criteria Deadline
CRITICAL High Likelihood & Catastrophic Operational Impact (Loss of separation, flight hazard). Immediate (72h)
HIGH Medium Likelihood & High Impact (Prolonged service disruption, data corruption). 30 days
MODERATE Medium Likelihood & Medium Impact (Policy violation, config drift). 90 days
LOW Low Likelihood & Low Impact (Documentation gaps, minor hardening deficit). 180+ days

Key Recommendations

  1. Mandate Safety/Cyber Integration: Formally implement the DO-326A risk process (SRAP), linking every cyber vulnerability to its maximum credible operational safety consequence.
  2. Implement Compensating Controls: Deploy protocol-aware Network Detection (NDR) tools to monitor ASTERIX/ADS-B traffic for spoofing.
  3. Establish Config Baselines: Define and document the "known good state" for all UNIX/Linux systems and use FIM tools to monitor for configuration drift.
  4. Strengthen Patch Validation: Update patch management to require verification using credentialed scan data (Nessus) to ensure patches are fully enabled, not just installed.
  5. Transition to Continuous Monitoring: Move from "snap-shot" assessments to a continuous improvement framework with regular DR testing and ongoing staff training.
