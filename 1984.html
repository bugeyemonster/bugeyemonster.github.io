Nineteen Eighty-Four

A summary of George Orwell's vision of a totalitarian future.

April 4, 1984
5 min read
W. Smith
Big Brother Is Watching

George Orwell's "Nineteen Eighty-Four" is a chilling dystopian novel published in 1949. It presents a vision of a totalitarian future where a single, all-powerful political party has complete control over every aspect of human life.

The World of Oceania

The story is set in Airstrip One (formerly Great Britain), a province of the superstate Oceania. The world is in a constant state of war, and society is governed by "The Party," which is led by a mysterious figure known as Big Brother. The Party maintains its power through a combination of constant surveillance, propaganda, and brutal psychological and physical manipulation.

"War is Peace. Freedom is Slavery. Ignorance is Strength."

The Rebellion of Winston Smith

The protagonist of the novel is Winston Smith, a low-ranking member of the Outer Party. Winston works in the Ministry of Truth, where his job is to rewrite historical records to align with the Party's ever-changing version of reality. Disillusioned with his oppressive existence and the Party's control over truth, Winston begins to secretly rebel. He starts by committing "thoughtcrime"—the act of holding unspoken beliefs that contradict the Party's ideology—and illegally purchases a diary to record his private thoughts.

Winston's rebellion escalates when he begins a clandestine affair with Julia, a free-spirited young woman who also works at the Ministry of Truth. Their relationship is a dangerous act of defiance, as personal loyalty and love are forbidden by the Party, which seeks to redirect all human emotion towards the state.

The Trap and Betrayal

Driven by a desire to overthrow the regime, Winston and Julia seek out the mythical "Brotherhood," an underground resistance movement supposedly led by Emmanuel Goldstein, the Party's arch-nemesis. Their search leads them to O'Brien, a charismatic and intelligent member of the Inner Party whom Winston believes is secretly part of the resistance.

However, O'Brien is actually a loyal agent of the Thought Police. Winston and Julia are inevitably captured and taken to the dreaded Ministry of Love, which is essentially the Party's torture and re-education center. Here, Winston is subjected to months of systematic torture by O'Brien, who aims not just to make Winston confess, but to completely break his spirit and reshape his perception of reality. The climax of his torture takes place in Room 101, where he is forced to confront his greatest fear.

The Final Victory

In the end, the Party succeeds. Winston is released back into society as a broken man, his independent spirit crushed. He no longer loves Julia; his mind has been completely reshaped, and he has learned to genuinely love Big Brother.

The novel concludes with the haunting image of Winston's complete and utter submission to the Party's absolute power.
